# PRODUCT.md — BodyForge Physical Therapy

## Register

**brand** — This is a marketing surface for a solo clinical practice. The design *is* the product: a
prospective patient's impression in the first five seconds decides whether they call.

## Product Purpose

An outpatient physical therapy clinic in Miami. Every session is one-to-one with the same licensed
clinician from evaluation to discharge.

**The practice is no longer one person (Aug 2026).** Dr. Perez-Espinosa founded it and still treats,
but there is now a clinical team, and a patient is matched to whichever clinician fits their case.
Copy must therefore promise *one-to-one attention with a named clinician you keep*, never "your
session is with Dr. Perez-Espinosa". He stays the credentialled face of the practice; he is not the
guaranteed provider. Named services already indexed by search ("One-on-One Pilates", the page
`<title>`) keep the "one-on-one" spelling; flowing prose uses "one-to-one".

**Verified claims only.** The live site supports: one-on-one sessions, evaluation by a clinical
specialist, exercise programming beginning on day one, manual therapy, outcome tracking against
measurable goals, and modalities used in support of treatment rather than as treatment. It does
**not** state session length, insurance status, patient volume, or success rates. None of those may
appear on the new site. **Session length is the one that keeps creeping back in**: "a full hour",
"an hour that is yours". Delete it on sight.

**Pricing is now partly published (Aug 2026), for Weekend Recovery only.** The clinic issued four
launch sheets, kept at `site/assets/img/promo/`, and every figure on `/weekend-recovery/` is
transcribed from them. There are three distinct audiences and they must not be blended: individuals,
first responders (police, fire, EMS, 911 dispatch, corrections) and partner gyms. Headline figures: $49 first session, $169/mo founding rate locked 12 months, first 25 members;
standard $85 single / $600 eight-pack / $199/mo; partner-gym $70 single and $60/visit on a four-pack
at $240; first responder $70 single, $240 four-pack, $160/mo and $280/mo. Standard performance
membership is $349/mo. Weekend hours Sat 7:00-14:00, Sun 13:00-19:00, by appointment. Do not round,
discount or extrapolate any of these, and do not invent a price for physical therapy, which is still
unpublished.

**The wellness disclaimer is not optional.** The first responder sheet carries it and it now appears
on the home page and the recovery page: *recovery sessions are wellness services and are not a
substitute for diagnosed physical therapy care.* It stays on any surface that sells a recovery
session.

The site's job, in priority order:

1. Get a qualified patient to book an evaluation.
2. Establish that the person treating you is a doctor with elite-level experience, not a chain.
3. Answer "do you treat my thing?" fast enough that the visitor doesn't leave to search again.
4. Rank for Miami-local physical therapy intent.

## Users

**Primary — the post-surgical adult (45–80).** Recently had a knee/hip/shoulder replacement or a
repair. Often referred, often researching on a phone in a parking lot. Anxious, comparison-shopping,
reads reviews first. Needs: proof of competence, a human face, a phone number, parking instructions.

**Secondary — the active adult / weekend athlete (25–45).** Runner's knee, rotator cuff, low back
pain. Wants to keep training through rehab, not be told to rest. Skeptical of "3 sets of 10 and a
heat pack." Needs: evidence, load, testing, return-to-sport criteria.

**Tertiary — the caregiver adult child.** Booking on behalf of a parent. Vetting hard. This is
literally the first testimonial on the current site.

## Brand

**Name:** BodyForge Physical Therapy
**Tagline (existing, keep):** Build Stronger. Live Better.
**Founder:** Dr. David Perez-Espinosa, PT, DPT. Program Director, Geriatrics Special Interest Group,
Florida Physical Therapy Association. Polestar-educated Pilates instructor. Has worked with elderly
patients, weekend warriors, NFL players, and UFC fighters.

**Voice words (physical objects, not adjectives):**
`calibrated` · `hands-on` · `load-bearing`

A torque wrench, not a scented candle. A goniometer, not a lotus flower. The brand's real
differentiator is **measurement**: they test, program, load, and retest. That is the story.

**Tone:** Direct, numerate, unhurried. Speaks to an adult. Never chirpy, never "wellness journey,"
never "we're passionate about helping you achieve your goals."

## Anti-references

Things this must not resemble:

- **Hospital-system healthcare web.** White + teal, rounded everything, stock photo of a smiling
  stranger in scrubs, "Patient Portal." This is the first-order category reflex. Reject.
- **Editorial-magazine landing page.** Display serif italic, small mono kickers, ruled columns,
  no imagery. This is the second-order reflex. Reject.
- **Supplement-brand gym site.** Condensed all-caps, neon-on-black, aggressive diagonal cuts,
  "UNLEASH YOUR POTENTIAL." Wrong audience; the primary patient is 68 and post-op.
- **Chiropractor SEO template.** Icon-title-text card grid, stock hands-on-back photo, phone number
  in a red banner.
- The current site: Astra starter theme, unedited, with three lorem-ipsum blog posts still live.

## Strategic Principles

1. **Measurement is the motif, without invented numbers.** The clinic tests, programs, loads and
   retests, and the design should read that way. But it has never published a patient measurement,
   so the motif is now the *instrument* (a graduated rule) rather than a *reading* (the retired
   goniometer arc and its illustrative degree values). See DESIGN.md.
2. **Show the actual clinic and the actual doctor.** Every photo on this site is a real photo of
   this room and this person. No stock. That is the trust asset a chain cannot buy.
3. **One page, fully answered.** The current site scatters thin content across eight pages. Content
   density is the fix, not more routes.
4. **The phone is the conversion path.** Dr. Perez-Espinosa asked for the booking form to be removed
   (Aug 2026): he would rather prospective patients call so he can hear the case before a time is
   agreed. Every primary button on every page is now `tel:+13054561004`. The `#book` anchor is kept
   on the contact section because the service pages and the old site link to it. Clinic email is
   `info@bodyforgept.com`; the old `Davidpe@` address is retired.
5. **The 68-year-old is the accessibility floor.** Type size, contrast, tap targets, and copy
   clarity are set by her, not by the 30-year-old runner.

## Constraints

- Real business facts only. Address, hours, phone, credentials, and testimonials are real and must
  stay accurate. Do not invent statistics, review counts, patient volumes, or outcomes.
- No insurance claims, and no physical therapy pricing: the business has never published either, and
  inventing them would be a factual claim about a real medical practice. Weekend Recovery pricing is
  the documented exception above.
- Scope of practice is the clinic's to define, not ours. The Neurological tab lists Parkinson's
  disease, foot drop and Ehlers-Danlos syndrome because the clinic grouped them that way. Ehlers-
  Danlos is a connective-tissue disorder rather than a neurological one in the strict sense; it was
  left where the clinic put it. Flag groupings, do not silently re-file them.
- Testimonial text and attributions are verbatim from the live site.
